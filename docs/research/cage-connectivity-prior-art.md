# Cage connectivity prior art

Serves the operator fork: how the connectivity of a coarse Catmull-Clark control cage is generated automatically for brush-tree (invariants cage-is-fitted, cage-bar, creases-declared/closed in `docs/brush-tree-target.md`). Prior-art pass per Eureka SKILL step 2. Facts with evidence pointers; "(inferred)" marks the only non-sourced remarks. Written 2026-10-12 by eyes-bt-cage-connectivity.

Read depth is stated per row. Full texts read: Hoppe 1994, Marinov & Kobbelt 2004 conference version, Lavoue & Dupont 2009, Ma et al. 2015 (author manuscript), Campen et al. 2012 (preprint), Viertel/Osting/Staten 2019 (grep-level on method and limitations). Abstract or repo README only: QuadWild paper (PDF hosts refused or blocked), Lavoue 2007 CGF, Fitmaps 2011, Ma 2004, Instant Meshes paper, Kloiber 2020 (not found).

## Headline facts

1. Every method here that hits a coarse cage obtains connectivity by one of four routes: (a) decimate a dense mesh (QEM or Hoppe edge collapse/swap/split) then optimise positions; (b) cluster/segment the target into regions and link boundary control points (Lavoue); (c) build a coarse quad layout from a cross field (Dual Loops, QuadWild, Viertel); (d) connect feature points (ridges/umbilics) by distance transform (Ma 2015). No source generates connectivity from the fitted limit surface alone; all start from a dense target mesh or point cloud.
2. Only route (a) with Hoppe's energy and route (b) with Lavoue declare creases as part of the output. Hoppe and Ma use Loop (triangles); Lavoue uses the Stam-Loop hybrid quad/triangle scheme; Fitmaps and the layout methods yield quads. None of the read papers targets Catmull-Clark creases on a quad-only cage at 200 faces.
3. The coarsest figures reported: Lavoue 2009 control meshes of 119 to 187 vertices (200 to 260 faces) for 5K to 15K-vertex scans; Ma 2015 239 to 383 vertices; Hoppe 1994 down to 87 vertices (a swept/NURBS source); Dual Loops 20 to 275 patches (layouts, not cages).
4. No read source treats thin wing tips or saddles as a named case. Failure modes recorded below are the ones the authors state.

## Notes by question

Creases and closed loops. Lavoue's boundary network is closed by construction (each region is bounded by a closed cycle of control polygons, Sec 5.2) and tags every inter-region edge sharp, with semi-sharp relaxation afterward. Hoppe leaves crease placement to a discrete edge-tag search with a per-edge penalty. Dual Loops and QuadWild take feature curves as input and force the layout to follow them; neither chooses which curves are creases.

Irregular vertex count. Marinov regularises valence by greedy edge flips on R(M) (Sec 3.3) and says the result is a local minimum. Lavoue explicitly accepts high valence and n-gons. Dual Loops ties irregular nodes to cross-field singularities and total curvature and reports node counts per model (Sec 7 table). Ma 2015 leaves non-regular vertices to a possible post-process.

Thin wings and saddles. No source above names them. Stated adjacent hazards: narrow patches (Campen Sec 5.3), thin regions and limit cycles near singularities (Viertel abstract and Sec 1), coarse-mesh lack of degrees of freedom for "tiny details" and larger maximal error (Lavoue Sec 7), and parameter-mapping flips at extremely coarse starting meshes (Marinov Sec 6). Ma 2015 notes planes and cylinders make every point a ridge point.

Bar match (inferred). Against the cage-bar (at most about 200 faces, at most 24% triangles, at most 62 irregular vertices): only Lavoue's published outputs are at that count (200 to 260 triangle-or-mixed faces, vertex counts 119 to 187), and his method states it does not optimise for quads or valence. No read source reports an irregular-vertex count for its control mesh; Dual Loops reports singularities per layout (16 to 83), which counts the irregular nodes of the layout.
